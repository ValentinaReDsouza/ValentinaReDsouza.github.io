n Systems, m Attackers

A Monte Carlo lab on the Law of Large Numbers and the Central Limit Theorem: how the total, average and normalized penetration scores behave as the number of systems grows.

1 · Setup: systems, attackers, score model

Each of the m attackers goes through the n systems one by one, and system i gives a penetration score Xᵢ (i.i.d.). Attacker a’s total is Sₙ = X₁+…+Xₙ. Running m attackers gives m independent samples of Sₙ, Sₙ/n and Sₙ/√n.

4 · Variance under the three normalizations

By linearity of expectation and independence of the \(X_i\), for any scaling \(c_n\):

\[\mathbb{E}[c_nS_n]=c_n\,n\mu,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(c_nS_n)=c_n^{2}\,n\sigma^{2}\]

Choosing \(c_n=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\) makes the variance constant:

\[\operatorname{Var}\!\left(\frac{S_n}{\sqrt n}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\right)^{2} n\sigma^{2}=\sigma^{2}\]

For comparison, \(c_n=1\) gives \(n\sigma^2\) (grows) and \(c_n=\frac1n\) gives \(\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\) (shrinks).

5 · LLN: convergence in probability of Sₙ/n

Plotted: the fraction of the \(m\) attackers with \(\left|\frac{S_n}{n}-\mu\right|>\varepsilon\), the Chebyshev bound, and the CLT estimate \(2\bigl(1-\Phi(\varepsilon\sqrt n/\sigma)\bigr)\).

Proof (Chebyshev):

\[P\!\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n}-\mu\right|\ge\varepsilon\right)\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}(S_n/n)}{\varepsilon^{2}}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{n\,\varepsilon^{2}}\;\xrightarrow{\,n\to\infty\,}\;0\]

6 · CLT and interpretation: n systems, m attackers

Rules of thumb for one attacker facing \(n\) systems, and for the best of \(m\) attackers:

\[\begin{aligned}S_n&\approx n\mu\pm\sigma\sqrt n &&\text{(total: spread grows)}\\[4pt]\frac{S_n}{n}&\approx\mu\pm\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n} &&\text{(average: spread shrinks)}\\[4pt]\max_{j\le m}S_n^{(j)}&\approx n\mu+\sigma\sqrt n\,\sqrt{2\ln m} &&\text{(best of } m \text{ attackers)}\end{aligned}\]

Central Limit Theorem: the two models

Rademacher (\(\mu=0,\ \sigma=1\)): the normalized score converges directly, \(\dfrac{S_n}{\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,1)\).

Bernoulli (\(\mu=p\neq0\)): \(\dfrac{S_n}{\sqrt n}\) has mean \(p\sqrt n\to+\infty\), so it must be centred first: \(\dfrac{S_n-np}{\sqrt{np(1-p)}}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,1)\). The rate of convergence is of order \(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt n}\).

Distance between the standardized score and N(0,1) (Kolmogorov): it falls like \(1/\sqrt n\) until it hits the Monte Carlo noise level (yellow).
