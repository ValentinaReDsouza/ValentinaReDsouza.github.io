Welfords Algorithm and Sampling Distributions

Knuth & Welford recurrences, numerical stability, and a Monte Carlo study of m attackers against n systems: sample means, sample variances, LLN and CLT.

1 · Exercise 1: Knuth & Welford recurrences

Knuth: online mean

\[\begin{aligned}\bar x_k&=\frac1k\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_i=\frac1k\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}x_i+x_k\Bigr)=\frac1k\bigl((k-1)\,\bar x_{k-1}+x_k\bigr)\\[4pt]&=\bar x_{k-1}+\frac{x_k-\bar x_{k-1}}{k}\end{aligned}\]

Welford: sum of squared differences

Define \(M_{2,k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(x_i-\bar x_k)^2\) and \(\delta_k=x_k-\bar x_{k-1}\). Knuth gives \(\bar x_k-\bar x_{k-1}=\delta_k/k\), hence \(x_k-\bar x_k=\delta_k\frac{k-1}{k}\).

Step 1. Separate the last term of the sum:

\[M_{2,k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x_i-\bar x_k)^2+(x_k-\bar x_k)^2\]

Step 2. For \(i=1,\dots,k-1\) substitute \(x_i-\bar x_k=(x_i-\bar x_{k-1})-\dfrac{\delta_k}{k}\) and expand the square:

\[\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x_i-\bar x_k)^2=\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x_i-\bar x_{k-1})^2-\frac{2\delta_k}{k}\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x_i-\bar x_{k-1})}_{=\,0}+(k-1)\frac{\delta_k^{2}}{k^{2}}\]

The cross term vanishes because the deviations from the mean \(\bar x_{k-1}\) sum to zero, and the first sum is \(M_{2,k-1}\). Hence

\[\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(x_i-\bar x_k)^2=M_{2,k-1}+(k-1)\frac{\delta_k^{2}}{k^{2}}\]

Step 3. The last term is \((x_k-\bar x_k)^2=\dfrac{(k-1)^2}{k^2}\,\delta_k^2\).

Step 4. Add the two pieces:

\[M_{2,k}=M_{2,k-1}+\frac{\delta_k^{2}}{k^{2}}\bigl[(k-1)+(k-1)^2\bigr]=M_{2,k-1}+\frac{k-1}{k}\,\delta_k^{2}\]

Step 5. Since \(x_k-\bar x_k=\delta_k\frac{k-1}{k}\), we have \((x_k-\bar x_{k-1})(x_k-\bar x_k)=\frac{k-1}{k}\delta_k^2\). Therefore

\[\boxed{M_{2,k}=M_{2,k-1}+(x_k-\bar x_{k-1})(x_k-\bar x_k)}\]

Unbiased sample variance (Bessel’s correction)

\[S_k^2=\frac{M_{2,k}}{k-1},\qquad \sum_{i}(X_i-\mu)^2=\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2+k(\bar X-\mu)^2\ \Rightarrow\ k\sigma^2=\mathbb E[M_{2,k}]+k\cdot\frac{\sigma^2}{k}\]

So \(\mathbb E[M_{2,k}]=(k-1)\sigma^2\) and \(\mathbb E[S_k^2]=\sigma^2\).

Core algorithm (the loop used by every simulation below)

count += 1;
let delta = x - mean;
mean += delta / count;
let delta2 = x - mean;
M2 += delta * delta2;        // variance = M2 / (count - 1)

5 · Impact of the sample size n

\[\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_n)=\frac{\sigma^2}{n},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(S_n^2)=\frac1n\Bigl(\mu_4-\frac{n-3}{n-1}\,\sigma^4\Bigr)\ \xrightarrow{\,n\to\infty\,}\ 0\]

Here \(\mu_4=\mathbb E[(X-\mu)^4]\). For a Gaussian \(\operatorname{Var}(S_n^2)=\frac{2\sigma^4}{n-1}\). Both variances go to 0, so \(\bar X\to\mu\) and \(S^2\to\sigma^2\) in probability (consistency).

6 · Theoretical Foundations & Key Findings

1. Why are these findings foundational for inferential statistics?

Unbiasedness: \(\mathbb E[\bar X]=\mu\) and \(\mathbb E[S^2]=\sigma^2\), so on average the estimators hit the target.

LLN: by Chebyshev, \(P(|\bar X_n-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2}\to0\): \(\bar X_n\to\mu\) in probability. The same holds for \(S_n^2\to\sigma^2\).

CLT: \(\sqrt n(\bar X_n-\mu)/\sigma\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,1)\) for any law with finite variance, so the sampling distribution is approximately normal even when the data are not.

Confidence intervals: because \(S\to\sigma\), Slutsky’s theorem gives \(\bar X\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\,S/\sqrt n\) as an asymptotic \((1-\alpha)\) interval for \(\mu\). Tests and intervals all rest on these three facts.

2. Why is S² an unbiased estimator of σ²? (Bessel’s correction)

Decompose \(\sum_i(X_i-\mu)^2=\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)^2+n(\bar X-\mu)^2\). Taking expectations: \(n\sigma^2=\mathbb E[M_2]+n\cdot\frac{\sigma^2}{n}\), hence \(\mathbb E[M_2]=(n-1)\sigma^2\).

The sample mean is fitted to the same data, so deviations from \(\bar X\) are systematically smaller than deviations from \(\mu\). Dividing by \(n\) would underestimate \(\sigma^2\) by the factor \(\frac{n-1}{n}\). Equivalently, the residuals satisfy one linear constraint \(\sum_i(X_i-\bar X)=0\), so only \(n-1\) degrees of freedom are left: divide by \(n-1\).

3. How does n affect the standard error of X̄ and of S²?

\(\mathrm{SE}(\bar X)=\sigma/\sqrt n\): to halve the error, quadruple n.

\(\operatorname{Var}(S^2)=\frac1n\bigl(\mu_4-\frac{n-3}{n-1}\sigma^4\bigr)\), so \(\mathrm{SE}(S^2)\approx\sigma^2\sqrt{\frac{2}{n-1}}\) in the Gaussian case. Both errors shrink like \(1/\sqrt n\), so both estimators are consistent. Estimating \(\sigma^2\) is harder than estimating \(\mu\) and depends on the fourth moment: heavy-tailed or zero-inflated threat scores need more data.

4. Why use Welford instead of the naive formula?

The naive formula subtracts two nearly equal large numbers when the mean is large compared with the spread (catastrophic cancellation), and can return zero or negative variance. Welford updates deviations from the running mean, so no huge numbers are subtracted. It also works in one pass on a stream, with constant memory.
