§1 · Task and vocabulary

§3 · Role of each standard and primitive

PieceRole / purpose
BIP-39Encodes 128–256 bits of entropy + a checksum (ENT/32 bits) as 12–24 words from a 2048-word list, then stretches it to a 512-bit seed with PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA512 (salt "mnemonic"+passphrase). The passphrase gives a "25th word".
BIP-32Hierarchical deterministic tree: HMAC-SHA512("Bitcoin seed", seed) → master key + chain code; child keys via CKD, hardened (i ≥ 2³¹, uses the parent private key) or normal (uses the parent public key). One backup, unlimited keys.
BIP-44Fixes the tree layout m / purpose' / coin_type' / account' / change / address_index. Ethereum: coin type 60 → m/44'/60'/0'/0/0, so different wallets recover the same accounts.
secp256k1Curve y² = x³ + 7 over Fp, p = 2²⁵⁶ − 2³² − 977, base point G of prime order n ≈ 2²⁵⁶. Public key Q = kG; ECDSA signatures prove ownership of k without revealing it.
Keccak-256Compresses the 64-byte public key (x‖y, no 04 prefix) into 32 bytes; the address is the last 20 bytes. Hashing hides nothing of k, it just yields a short, uniform identifier.
EIP-55Hex digit i of the address is uppercased if letter and nibble i of Keccak-256(lowercase address) is ≥ 8. Costs no extra characters, detects typos with probability ≈ 1 − 2−15.

§5 · Keccak-256 vs SHA3-256 vs SHA-2

SHA-2/SHA-512 (2001) is a Merkle–Damgård design with a compression function; BIP-39 and BIP-32 use it through PBKDF2/HMAC. Keccak is a sponge construction (rate 1088, capacity 512 for the 256-bit variant) that won the NIST SHA-3 competition in 2012. When NIST standardised SHA-3 (FIPS 202, 2015) it added domain-separation bits, changing the padding byte from 0x01 (original Keccak) to 0x06. Ethereum had already adopted the original Keccak-256, so Keccak-256 ≠ SHA3-256, even though the permutation is identical.

SHA-2 (SHA-256/512)Keccak / SHA-3
ConstructionMerkle–Damgård (compression function block by block)Sponge: absorb into a 1600-bit state, then squeeze
Standardised2001–2002 (FIPS 180-2)Contest winner 2012, FIPS 202 in 2015
PaddingKeccak-256: 0x01…0x80 · SHA3-256: 0x06…0x80 — same permutation, different output
Length extensionPossible on plain SHA-256/512Not possible
Role in the pipelineSHA-256: BIP-39 checksum · SHA-512: PBKDF2, HMAC (seed, BIP-32)Keccak-256: address and EIP-55

§8 · EIP-55 checksum validator

Try changing the case of one letter, or one digit: the checksum is a function of the address's own hash, so a typo flips about half of the case bits and is almost surely caught. An all-lowercase address is valid but carries no protection.

§10 · Why not one single algorithm?

Because the steps need incompatible properties: BIP-39/32/44 need deterministic, key-separating derivation and a recoverable layout (a KDF/MAC is enough, no algebra); secp256k1 needs an algebraic one-way map with a public/private structure that supports signatures — a hash cannot sign; Keccak-256 needs to be a short, uniform, collision-resistant identifier of an arbitrary-size input (curve points are not uniform, Chart 3–5); EIP-55 only needs to detect human errors cheaply, and is useless against adversaries. Each layer also shields the previous: the address hides the public key until the first spend, the seed hides all keys, the passphrase hides the seed. Chronologically they were designed separately (1977 → 2016) by different communities and composed rather than redesigned.

PieceProblemNeedsWhy the others cannot do it
BIP-39/32/44Back up and organise many keysDeterminism, human-sized backup, fixed layoutConventions built from SHA-256, HMAC, PBKDF2; curve or hash alone has no tree
secp256k1Prove control of an addressAsymmetric pair + signaturesA hash has no key pair and cannot verify a signature
Keccak-256Short uniform identifierOne-way, fixed size, avalancheCurve points have algebraic structure and are not uniform
EIP-55Catch typosError detection without changing the 20 bytesA hash has no place in the address to sit: the letter case is free storage

Separation is also a safety property: a flaw in one primitive hurts only its own job (a wrong checksum exposes no key).

2 · Six notions, one line each

NotionAnswer
Key generationCreates new randomness, once, at the start (the entropy behind the mnemonic). Nothing earlier determines it.
Key derivationDeterministic: from an existing secret plus context the same keys always come out (PBKDF2, HMAC, CKD).
Public-key cryptographyKey pair with a one-way link k → Q; gives signatures (secp256k1).
HashingAny input → fixed-size digest; no key, no secret, one-way (Keccak-256, SHA-256/512).
ChecksumA few redundant bits that reveal accidental errors; not secret, no defence against an attacker (BIP-39 last bits, EIP-55).
EncodingSame information, different representation; reversible by anyone (words, hex, 0x, letter case).

4 · Synthesis

Chronology. RSA (1977) → SHA-2 (2001) → BIP-32 (2012) → BIP-39 (2013) → BIP-44 (2014) → SHA-3 (2015) → EIP-55 (2016): the mathematical tools are old, the wallet standards are young (§4).

SHA-2 vs Keccak. SHA-2 is Merkle–Damgård, Keccak/SHA-3 is a sponge. Ethereum uses the original Keccak-256 (padding 0x01), not the standardised SHA3-256 (0x06): same permutation, different digest (§5).

Why several techniques. Each step needs a different property: BIP-39/32/44 deterministic, recoverable key management; secp256k1 a key pair with signatures; Keccak-256 a short uniform irreversible identifier; EIP-55 cheap error detection. No single algorithm has all of them, and separation limits the damage of a flaw in one piece (§10).

5 · Osservazioni dalle prove

  • Avalanche (§6). Con 200, 1000 e 3000 prove Keccak-256, SHA3-256 e SHA-256 danno sempre una media ≈ 128 bit (da 127,4 a 128,6) e σ ≈ 8 (da 7,4 a 8,3): più prove riducono solo il rumore. Keccak e SHA3-256 sono statisticamente indistinguibili, cambia solo il digest. Il controllo XOR cambia sempre esattamente 2 bit (σ = 0): non è un hash.
  • Indirizzi (§7). Con N = 100, 300 e 1000 nessun bit su 160 esce da ±3σ (ne attendevo ≈ 0,4), χ² = 9,7 / 12,1 / 14,1 (sempre sotto 25) e le lettere medie sono ≈ 14,9 (attese 15). Aumentando N la banda si stringe (σ = 0,5/√N: da 0,050 a 0,016) e gli indirizzi restano compatibili con stringhe uniformi.
  • Checksum (§12). Con 500, 2000 e 5000 prove BIP-39 accetta circa il 6 % degli errori (parola cambiata 5,9–6,5 %, due parole scambiate 6,0–7,0 %), vicino a 1/16 = 6,25 % (4 bit). EIP-55 con una cifra sbagliata: 0 su 500, 0 su 2000, 1 su 5000 (atteso ≈ 2−15, cioè ≈ 0,15 casi); un solo cambio di maiuscola non passa mai (0 su 5000). Il checksum dell'indirizzo protegge circa 2000 volte meglio di quello della frase, ma un indirizzo tutto minuscolo non protegge affatto.
  • Entropia (§9). A 103 tentativi/s 12 parole richiedono 1027,7 anni; anche a 1018/s restano 1012,7 anni, più dell'età dell'universo (1010,15). Da 12 a 24 parole la barra cresce di circa 38 ordini di grandezza, ma oltre 128 bit il limite è la curva: 12 parole bastano già.
  • Pipeline (§2). La frase di test dà 0x9858… (i = 0), 0x6Fac… (i = 1), 0xb671… (i = 2): nessuna relazione visibile tra gli indirizzi. Lo stesso indice 1 in stile Ledger Live (cambia l'account) dà 0x7883…: stessa frase, convenzione diversa, indirizzo diverso. Con la passphrase "hello" il primo indirizzo diventa 0x8fde… senza alcun errore.
  • Checksum e seed. Con 12 × "abandon" il checksum BIP-39 è non valido, ma la pagina deriva comunque un indirizzo (0x362A…): il seed dipende solo dal testo, il controllo serve a chi digita la frase. Le 500 frasi generate (12 e 24 parole) sono risultate tutte valide.
  • Keccak ≠ SHA3-256. Sulla stringa vuota danno c5d24601… e a7ffc6f8…: stessa permutazione, padding 0x01 contro 0x06, digest completamente diversi.

I valori casuali (avalanche, statistiche, checksum) cambiano un poco a ogni esecuzione; gli indirizzi sono fissi.

In one sentence. Randomness is generated once, everything after it is derivation, the curve links secret to public, the hash shortens the public key to an address, and the checksum and the encodings only make it safe and easy for humans to copy.
